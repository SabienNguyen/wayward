    How chess works

    Two players, an 8×8 board, 16 pieces each. White moves first. You win by checkmating the enemy king: attacking it so that no move can save it.

    1. The pieces

    Tap a piece to study how it moves.

    2. Check and checkmate
    • A king that is attacked is in check. You must get out of check at once: move the king, capture the attacker, or block the line.
    • You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.
    • If you're in check and nothing gets you out, it's checkmate and the game is over.
    3. Special moves
    • Castling: the king moves two squares toward a rook, and the rook jumps to the other side of it. Allowed only if neither has moved, the squares between are empty, and the king isn't in check, doesn't pass through an attacked square, and doesn't land on one.
    • En passant: if an enemy pawn moves two squares and lands beside your pawn, you may capture it as if it had moved one square, but only on the very next move.
    • Promotion: a pawn reaching the last rank becomes a queen, rook, bishop or knight of your choice.
    4. Draws
    • Stalemate: the side to move has no legal move but isn't in check.
    • Threefold repetition: the same position occurs three times.
    • Fifty-move rule: fifty moves by each side with no capture and no pawn move.
    • Insufficient material: neither side can possibly checkmate, for example king against king and bishop.
    5. Notation

    Files are a–h from White's left, ranks 1–8 from White's side. A move is the piece letter (K Q R B N; none for pawns) plus the square: Nf3. x marks a capture (exd5), + check, # checkmate, O-O and O-O-O castling, =Q promotion.
